Chattanooga AI Roundtable

The Future of Work

A private dinner for a small group of Chattanooga-area executives. No pitch, no panel — just an honest conversation about how work, on the factory floor and in the back office, is actually changing once the noise dies down.

No Pitch. Just Dinner, and an Honest Conversation About AI.

Join Josh Patterson (Patterson Consulting), Dr. Mina Sartipi (Center for Urban Informatics and Progress, UT Chattanooga), and Dr. Scott Worland (CTO, Norm.ai) for an intimate dinner with a small group of Chattanooga-area executives. One table, one evening — half practitioners who build AI systems for a living, half operators running businesses where those systems are landing.

The format is intentional. Half the table brings deep technical context — AI research, smart city systems, and the infrastructure behind high-stakes legal and compliance agents. The other half brings operating experience across manufacturing, healthcare, financial services, construction, and aerospace. Both sides are working through the same question: after the hype, what does AI actually change about how work gets done?

For 250 years the pattern has held: the handloom weaver's job disappeared; weaving did not. Jobs change but work remains. Routine work compresses, and the judgment and relationships that remain become the job. The question for every leader at the table is the same — how do you restructure roles upward instead of simply cutting them?

What to expect:

  • A table split between AI practitioners and industry operators — not a panel for one, not a pitch to the other
  • Perspectives from healthcare, manufacturing, finance, construction, and aerospace alongside AI, smart city, and legal tech backgrounds
  • No slides, no sales, no vendor pitch
  • Candid, off-the-record conversation among peers
  • Dinner and drinks, on us

Location: The Lookout Mountain Country Club, Chattanooga, TN

What We'll Dig Into

Four threads, one table. The through-line: automation reshapes work rather than erasing it — and the organizations that understand how win.

01

Manufacturing & Automation

What LLMs and automation actually do to manufacturing work — task by task, wage by wage — and why the honest story is role transformation, not wholesale replacement.

02

Knowledge Work, Restructured

Routine execution compresses; the judgment, exceptions, and relationships that remain move up the value chain and become the job. The layered view of how cognitive work actually changes.

03

Why AGI Won't Save You

"We can know more than we can tell." The judgment layer resists codification — which is exactly why the AGI-arrives-with-the-answers story keeps missing, and what that means for how you plan.

04

Managing the Transition

The real risk isn't automation — it's speed and concentration. How leaders elevate roles instead of eliminating them, and keep a workforce moving with the change instead of against it.
